## Purpose

Every stock trades on a story. The market's dominant narrative about a company shapes how investors interpret the same fundamental data — and a narrative shift can compress or expand multiples faster than any change in earnings. A company re-rated from "growth story" to "broken story" can see a 50% multiple compression even if revenues are flat. Conversely, a turnaround company newly embraced as a "recovery story" can re-rate significantly before fundamentals fully materialize. Identifying the current narrative, evaluating its health, and anticipating what could change it is one of the most important — and most underused — analytical inputs in equity research.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Classify the Dominant Narrative Category

Identify which of the following story categories most accurately describes how the market currently frames this company. The narrative category determines which metrics investors focus on, what multiples they apply, and what news is considered signal vs. noise.

| Narrative Category | Description | Typical Investor Focus | Multiple Basis |
|-------------------|-------------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Growth Story** | Company growing revenues/market share at above-market rate with clear path to scale | Revenue growth rate, TAM, NRR/NDR, product expansion | Revenue multiple or forward P/E with growth premium |
| **Value Play** | Company trading below intrinsic value with catalyst for recognition | FCF yield, P/B, asset coverage, buyback yield | P/E, EV/EBITDA vs. peers |
| **Turnaround** | Declining or troubled company with credible operational recovery plan | New management execution, margin progression, debt paydown | Discounted recovery — P/E or EV/EBITDA on normalized earnings |
| **Yield Vehicle** | Stable, mature company primarily valued for income distribution | Dividend yield, payout ratio, coverage ratio, yield vs. 10-year | Yield-based; dividend discount or peer yield comparison |
| **Momentum Trade** | Stock moving on technical or sentiment momentum with narrative as justification | Price action, relative strength, options positioning | Price target based on momentum target, less fundamental anchor |
| **Broken Story** | Narrative has collapsed; market no longer believes the original thesis | Damage control metrics, liquidity, restructuring viability | Distressed valuation — asset coverage, liquidation value |

A company can exhibit characteristics of multiple categories — identify the primary narrative and note any secondary narrative being layered on.

### Step 2: Assess Narrative Health

Evaluate whether the current narrative is intact, evolving, or under stress using three signals:

**Signal A — Evidence Coherence**: Does recent news, earnings data, and management commentary reinforce or contradict the narrative?
- Intact: recent data confirms the story (growth company posting acceleration, turnaround posting margin expansion)
- Evolving: narrative shifting but direction is clear and credible (yield vehicle adding growth segment)
- Broken: recent data directly contradicts the narrative without credible management response

**Signal B — Market Behavior**: Is the stock responding to the narrative category's relevant metrics as expected?
- Intact: growth stock rising on revenue beat even with margin miss; value stock rising on buyback announcement
- Distorted: growth stock falling on revenue beats — market no longer believes growth is sustainable
- Transition: market rewarding metrics from a different narrative category (turnaround stock rising on yield announcement — narrative shifting toward income)

**Signal C — Analyst Consensus Framing**: How are sell-side analysts framing the investment case in recent notes?
- Intact: analysts using language consistent with the dominant narrative; price targets anchored to appropriate multiple basis
- Evolving: analyst notes introducing new framing, changing their multiple basis
- Conflicted: analysts using different narrative frameworks — half valuing on growth, half on yield — indicates transition

Classify narrative health as: **INTACT / EVOLVING / BROKEN**

### Step 3: Evaluate Narrative Shift Risk and Multiple Impact

Narrative shifts are the largest source of multiple compression or expansion. For each plausible narrative shift scenario, estimate the multiple impact:

- **Growth Story → Value Play**: occurs when revenue deceleration is confirmed. Multiple can compress from 10-15x revenue to 15-25x earnings. Downside can be 30-60% on high-multiple names.
- **Growth Story → Broken Story**: occurs when TAM is disproven, competition destroys moat, or fraud surfaces. Multiple collapse — often 50-80% drawdown.
- **Turnaround → Growth Story**: occurs when recovery exceeds expectations. Multiple expansion from trough (5-8x EBITDA) to sector average (10-15x EBITDA).
- **Value Play → Broken Story**: occurs when "value" was actually a value trap — deteriorating business without a catalyst. Market shifts from P/E to distressed valuation.
- **Yield Vehicle → Broken Story**: occurs when dividend is cut. Yield investors exit simultaneously; stock often falls more than the dividend reduction implies.

Identify the most likely adverse narrative shift and the trigger that would cause it.

## Key Questions

1. What single piece of news or data would most damage the current narrative — and how likely is it over the next 12 months?
2. Is the market applying a multiple consistent with the stated narrative category, or is there a mismatch (applying growth multiples to a turnaround — stretched)?
3. Has the narrative shifted recently without a corresponding price adjustment — a lagging re-rating risk?
4. Is management language reinforcing the narrative or defensively distancing from it?
5. Are there competing narratives among analysts? Competing narrative environments create volatility as the market moves between framing.
6. What event would accelerate narrative adoption from minority to consensus view — either bull or bear narrative?

## Red Flags

- Company is priced as a growth story but revenue growth has decelerated for 3+ consecutive quarters — narrative-valuation mismatch
- Management adopting defensive language ("right-sizing," "focusing on profitability") while narrative still classified as growth — early broken story signal
- Sell-side analysts split between two incompatible narrative categories — market has not yet resolved which story wins
- Narrative depends on a single catalyst (regulatory approval, product launch, contract win) that has not yet materialized and has been delayed
- Turnaround narrative showing no margin or revenue progress after 4+ quarters — credibility erosion
- Momentum trade narrative with no underlying fundamental support — momentum reversal risk is binary
- A previously niche bearish narrative (short-seller thesis, regulatory risk) is gaining mainstream analyst attention — narrative shift underway

## Source Requirements

- **Sell-side research notes**: Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, sector boutiques — Tier 2 (framing and language most valuable; price targets less so)
- **Earnings call transcripts**: management tone, language shifts, guidance framing — Tier 1 (company-originated; most direct narrative signal from the source)
- **Investor day / conference presentations**: long-form narrative articulation — Tier 1 when available
- **Financial media framing**: WSJ, FT, Bloomberg feature coverage — Tier 2 (reflects mainstream narrative adoption; a WSJ feature on a bear case means narrative has reached mass audience)
- **Short seller reports**: Hindenburg, Muddy Waters, Citron — Tier 2 (competing narrative; validates if mainstream media amplifies)

**Tier classification note**: Market narrative analysis is entirely qualitative. It is not scored numerically. Its function is to contextualize why a given multiple exists and flag when that multiple is at risk of re-rating. Never use narrative alone to justify buying or selling — use it to size conviction and anticipate multiple expansion/compression risk.

## Output

- Dominant narrative category: GROWTH STORY / VALUE PLAY / TURNAROUND / YIELD VEHICLE / MOMENTUM TRADE / BROKEN STORY
- Secondary narrative (if present): classification and description
- Narrative health assessment: INTACT / EVOLVING / BROKEN with supporting evidence from all three signals
- Multiple basis currently applied: what metric drives the current valuation (revenue multiple, P/E, yield, EV/EBITDA)
- Narrative-multiple alignment: ALIGNED (narrative supports current multiple) / STRETCHED (narrative implies lower multiple) / DISCOUNTED (narrative implies higher multiple)
- Most likely adverse narrative shift: description of the shift scenario and triggering event
- Estimated multiple compression from adverse shift: percentage range based on narrative category transition analysis
- Narrative risk classification: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH
