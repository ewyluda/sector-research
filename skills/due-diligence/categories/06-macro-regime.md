## Purpose

Determine how the current macroeconomic backdrop affects the investment thesis. Macro conditions don't change the quality of a business, but they change the price the market is willing to pay and the timing of when that quality gets recognized. A great company in a deteriorating macro regime can still be a poor near-term investment — and a cyclical company perfectly positioned in an early recovery can generate outsized returns despite mediocre fundamentals.

## Key Dimensions

| Dimension | Sub-Skill | Core Question |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Rate Environment | rate-environment | How do current and expected interest rates affect valuation and business economics? |
| Inflation Cycle | inflation-cycle | Where is inflation in its cycle, and can the company protect or expand margins? |
| Yield Curve | yield-curve | What does the curve shape signal about growth expectations and recession probability? |
| Sector Rotation | sector-rotation | Which sectors historically outperform the current phase of the economic cycle? |
| Regime Classification | regime-classification | Is the market in a risk-on, risk-off, or transition regime, and how should positioning reflect that? |

## Synthesis

After running all sub-skills, answer these integrative questions:
1. Is the macro backdrop **tailwind, neutral, or headwind** for this investment over the next 12-24 months?
2. Does the sector and company positioning **match the current cycle phase**, or is the thesis fighting macro?
3. What macro **regime shift** would most damage the investment thesis, and how likely is it?

## Scoring

**MR Score (0-100)** = Rate impact (25%) + Inflation impact (20%) + Yield curve signal (20%) + Sector rotation alignment (20%) + Regime classification (15%)

Rate impact scoring: Rates falling/low and company benefits = 80-100; neutral or mixed = 40-60; rates rising and company exposed = 0-30.
Inflation impact scoring: Inflation falling and company has pricing power = 80-100; neutral = 40-60; inflation rising and company absorbs cost = 0-30.
Yield curve scoring: Normal/steepening = 70-100; flat = 40-60; inverted or inverting = 0-30.
Sector rotation scoring: Sector historically outperforms current cycle phase = 70-100; mixed = 40-60; historically underperforms = 0-30.
Regime scoring: Clear risk-on with company positioned well = 80-100; transition = 40-60; risk-off = 0-30.

## Red Flags

- Investment thesis depends on macro assumptions that contradict forward market pricing (futures, breakevens)
- High-duration/growth company in a rising-rate environment with no margin of safety in valuation
- Sector historically underperforms during the current cycle phase with no differentiation thesis
- Yield curve has been inverted for 6+ months (elevated recession probability in 6-18 month window)
- Steepening after inversion — the most reliable historical recession confirmation signal
- Macro regime is RISK-OFF or TRANSITION and position sizing has not been adjusted accordingly
- Company has no pricing power in a rising-inflation environment

## Output

- MR Score (0-100) with sub-factor breakdown
- Rate environment assessment: direction, pace, real vs. nominal impact on valuation
- Inflation cycle position: RISING / PEAKING / FALLING / BOTTOMING with margin impact
- Yield curve signal: spread levels, inversion status, recession probability estimate
- Sector rotation alignment: cycle phase vs. sector historical performance
- Regime classification: RISK-ON / RISK-OFF / TRANSITION with confidence level
- Macro tailwind/headwind summary and recommended position sizing adjustment
