## Purpose

Executive compensation is the clearest expression of what a board actually values, regardless of what they say at Investor Day. When compensation is tightly linked to shareholder returns, management tends to think and behave like owners. When compensation is primarily cash-based with weak performance linkage, management is effectively an employee extracting value from the business regardless of outcomes.

The core questions are: Is the CEO paid to create value, or just to show up? Do the incentive structures reward the same things shareholders care about?

## Methodology

**Step 1 — Total compensation benchmarking**

Pull the CEO's total direct compensation (TDC) from the proxy statement (DEF 14A) for the trailing 3 years. TDC includes: base salary, annual bonus (cash), long-term incentive (LTI) — typically stock options and RSUs — and all other compensation.

Calculate the CEO's percentile rank within the disclosed peer group:
- Find the compensation peer group (disclosed in the proxy — typically 15-25 companies)
- Pull each peer company's CEO TDC from their respective proxy filings
- Rank the subject CEO vs. the peer group at 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles

Benchmark separately: base salary percentile, annual bonus percentile, and LTI percentile. A high-salary, low-LTI structure indicates cash extraction preference over equity alignment.

**Step 2 — Pay-for-performance correlation**

Compare year-over-year changes in CEO total compensation against year-over-year changes in:
- Total shareholder return (TSR) vs. compensation peer group TSR
- Adjusted EBITDA or operating income (the metric most commonly used in annual bonus formulas)
- Revenue growth
- Return on invested capital (ROIC) — often the most important long-term metric

Calculate the pay-for-performance alignment for the trailing 3 years:
- If TSR declines 20% and CEO compensation rises 15%, that is a serious misalignment
- If TSR rises 40% and CEO compensation rises 25%, that reflects appropriate sharing of value creation
- If compensation is stable regardless of outcomes, alignment is weak

Review the compensation committee's rationale: did they exercise discretion to reduce or increase awards based on non-financial performance or "strategic accomplishments"? Discretionary adjustments upward during underperformance are a significant red flag.

**Step 3 — Equity vs. cash mix**

Calculate for the CEO and the named executive officer (NEO) group:
- LTI % of total direct compensation: equity-heavy (>60% LTI) = aligned; cash-heavy (<40% LTI) = extractive
- Within LTI, distinguish between performance share units (PSUs) with vesting conditions tied to performance metrics vs. time-vested RSUs (no performance link)
- Performance-contingent equity (PSUs): what metrics trigger vesting? Common: relative TSR, EPS growth, ROIC improvement
- Time-vested RSUs: vesting on a schedule regardless of performance — alignment is weaker but retention-oriented

Classify LTI structure:
- STRONG: >50% of LTI is performance-contingent (PSUs with rigorous, pre-set targets)
- MODERATE: 30-50% PSUs; remainder time-vested
- WEAK: <30% PSUs; predominantly time-vested RSUs or stock options without performance conditions

**Step 4 — Governance provisions review**

Review the proxy for the following alignment provisions:
- **Stock ownership requirements**: Does the CEO have a minimum stock ownership guideline (e.g., 5x base salary)? Are current holdings in compliance?
- **Clawback provisions**: Can the board claw back compensation if it is later determined to have been based on misstated financials or misconduct? (Required by SEC Rule 10D-1 since 2023 — but quality of implementation varies)
- **Holding requirements**: Must executives hold shares for a minimum period after vesting (post-vest holding requirements)?
- **Anti-hedging and anti-pledging policies**: Are executives prohibited from hedging or pledging their equity holdings? Pledging creates forced-sale risk.
- **Cooling-off periods**: Are there robust blackout windows that prevent opportunistic trading around results?

## Key Questions

1. Is the CEO's total compensation in the top quartile while total shareholder returns are in the bottom quartile over the same trailing 3-year period?
2. What percentage of the CEO's long-term incentive is performance-contingent — and are the performance targets set at genuinely challenging levels or set to pay out regardless?
3. Has the compensation committee exercised upward discretion to increase awards during years of underperformance?
4. Does the CEO own meaningful shares purchased in the open market, or does their ownership consist entirely of vested equity grants?
5. Are the performance metrics in the annual bonus and LTI plans the same ones that create shareholder value, or are they easier-to-hit operational metrics disconnected from returns?

## Red Flags

- CEO total compensation in the >75th percentile of peers with total shareholder returns below the 25th percentile over the same period
- Compensation increasing year-over-year during periods of declining TSR, earnings, or ROIC
- Annual bonus plan with targets retroactively adjusted or set at below-prior-year performance levels (goal posts moved)
- LTI composed entirely of time-vested RSUs with no performance conditions — executives are compensated for tenure, not value creation
- No clawback policy or a policy too narrow to cover material misconduct
- CEO equity holdings primarily from unvested grants rather than open-market purchases — no personal financial risk
- Compensation peer group manipulated to include larger companies to justify above-market pay
- Anti-hedging policies that permit hedging of unvested equity (eliminates downside risk without the board's awareness)
- Board compensation committee with no compensation expertise and high CEO influence over director nominations

## Source Requirements

**Tier 1 (required for this analysis):**
- SEC EDGAR: DEF 14A (proxy statement) — CD&A (Compensation Discussion and Analysis), Summary Compensation Table, peer group disclosure, performance targets and achievement
- Peer company DEF 14A filings: for benchmarking purposes
- SEC EDGAR: Form 4 (insider transactions) — to assess open-market purchases vs. grant-only ownership

**Tier 2 (qualitative context only):**
- ISS and Glass Lewis proxy advisor reports (if available): governance ratings and pay-for-performance assessments
- News: shareholder advisory votes on executive compensation (Say on Pay votes below 70% approval signal investor concern)
- Compensation consultant disclosures in proxy: who advised the committee, and do they have conflicts?

## Scoring

**Compensation Alignment Score (0-100)**

| Factor | Weight | Score Bands |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Pay-for-performance correlation (comp change vs. TSR change, 3yr) | 35% | Tight positive correlation = 80-100; Moderate = 50-70; Inverse (comp up/TSR down) = 0-30 |
| CEO comp percentile vs. TSR percentile gap | 25% | Comp ≤ TSR percentile = 80-100; Comp 0-25pts above TSR pctile = 50-70; Comp >25pts above TSR pctile = 0-40 |
| LTI structure (performance-contingent %) | 25% | >60% PSUs = 80-100; 40-60% = 55-75; <40% = 0-50 |
| Governance provisions (ownership, clawback, anti-hedging) | 15% | All 3 in place with robust terms = 85-100; 2 of 3 = 55-75; <2 or weak terms = 0-45 |

Score ranges:
- 80-100: ALIGNED — compensation structure creates genuine owner-operator mentality
- 60-79: MIXED — adequate alignment in some dimensions, gaps in others
- 40-59: WEAKLY ALIGNED — performance linkage exists but is insufficient or easily gamed
- 0-39: MISALIGNED — executives are rewarded for presence, not performance

## Output

- Compensation Alignment Score (0-100) with sub-factor breakdown
- Compensation alignment rating: ALIGNED / MIXED / MISALIGNED
- CEO total direct compensation vs. peer group (3-year table with percentile rank)
- Pay-for-performance correlation chart (CEO comp change vs. TSR change, trailing 3 years)
- LTI structure breakdown (PSUs vs. time-vested RSUs vs. options, as % of total LTI)
- Governance provisions checklist (ownership requirements, clawback, anti-hedging, holding requirements)
- Say on Pay vote history (trailing 3 years, % approval)
- Top compensation governance risk and recommended shareholder action
