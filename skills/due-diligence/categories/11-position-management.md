## Purpose

Analysis that does not translate into action has no value. Position management is the bridge between a validated thesis and an actual investment. It answers four questions that no amount of additional research can answer: where to enter, how large to go, when to exit regardless of price, and what to watch between now and exit.

Unlike the analysis phases that precede it, position management is not about gathering more evidence — the evidence phase is complete. It is about converting a conviction level and risk tolerance into a structured execution plan that can be followed without emotional override when the position moves against you.

Position management decisions made before entry are orders of magnitude better than decisions made during drawdown. A pre-committed stop-loss is discipline; a stop-loss decided during a 20% drawdown is capitulation rationalized as risk management.

## Synthesis

After completing all sub-skills, the position plan is complete when it can answer:
1. "At what price and under what market conditions would I initiate this position today?"
2. "If I entered at $X, how much would I lose at maximum, and is that loss acceptable given portfolio context?"
3. "What single piece of news or data would cause me to exit the entire position by end of day, regardless of price?"
4. "What am I checking each quarter, and what response does each outcome trigger?"

A plan that cannot answer all four is incomplete. Do not enter the position until all four have specific, written answers.

## Scoring

**No composite score for Position Management.** This is an execution category, not an analysis category.

The preceding analysis phases (Business Quality through Thesis Construction) produce scores that feed into position sizing conviction adjustments. Position Management uses those scores as inputs to sizing decisions but does not generate its own score. An investor who generates another composite score here is still in analysis mode, not execution mode.

The output of this category is a position plan document — a set of pre-committed decisions — not a number on a scale.

## Red Flags

- Entry zone defined as a single price rather than a zone — implies false precision and makes execution conditional on hitting an exact print
- Exit strategy that relies entirely on price target achievement without thesis invalidation triggers — leaves the investor with no exit protocol if the thesis fails before the target is reached
- Position size that exceeds 10% of portfolio for a single equity, regardless of conviction level — concentration risk that survives even a high-conviction thesis
- Stop-loss defined purely by price decline percentage without any connection to fundamental events — arbitrary stops that do not distinguish noise from signal
- No monitoring cadence — "I'll check when something happens" is not a plan; it is reactive management
- Monitoring list that contains more than 10 signals — cognitive overload means nothing gets properly monitored
- Response protocol that says "investigate" for every signal type — investigation without pre-committed response thresholds is analysis paralysis
- Time horizon undefined or set to "indefinitely" — an open-ended horizon removes all temporal discipline from position review

## Output

- Entry zone with price boundaries and conditions for initiating (not a single target price)
- Three exit triggers with specific, pre-committed criteria (thesis invalidation, price target, opportunity cost)
- Position size as a percentage of portfolio with conviction adjustment applied
- Stop-loss level with both price-based and fundamental-based components
- Monitoring signal list (maximum 10 signals) with response protocol for each
- Quarterly review checklist tied to earnings and filing calendar
- One-page position summary: thesis in one sentence, entry zone, size, stops, time horizon, top 3 monitoring signals
