## Purpose

Determine whether the company has sustainable competitive advantages that protect it from competition and margin erosion. A moat is not just a competitive advantage — it's one that is durable, meaning it persists or widens over time without constant reinvestment.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Identify Moat Type(s)

Evaluate the company against five moat types. Most strong businesses have 1-2 primary moats; 3+ is exceptional.

**Network Effects** — Does the product become more valuable as more people use it?
- Direct network effects: each user makes the product better for other users (social platforms, marketplaces)
- Indirect network effects: more users attract more complementors, which attract more users (app stores, payment networks)
- Evidence: user growth compounds without proportional marketing spend; competitors cannot reach critical mass

**Switching Costs** — How painful is it for customers to leave?
- Financial switching costs: contractual obligations, migration expenses, retraining
- Procedural switching costs: workflow integration, data lock-in, habit formation
- Relational switching costs: trust, institutional knowledge, relationship depth
- Evidence: high retention rates (>90%), long contract durations, low churn even during downturns

**Cost Advantages** — Can the company produce at lower cost than competitors?
- Scale economies: fixed costs spread over larger volume
- Process advantages: proprietary technology or methodology that lowers unit costs
- Resource access: preferential access to inputs, distribution, or talent
- Evidence: structurally higher margins than peers with similar products

**Intangible Assets** — Does the company own non-replicable assets?
- Brand: pricing power derived from reputation (customers pay more for the same functional product)
- Intellectual property: patents, trade secrets, regulatory licenses
- Regulatory barriers: licenses, approvals, or certifications that limit competition
- Evidence: price premium vs. generic alternatives; barriers to new entrants

**Efficient Scale** — Is the market too small for another competitor?
- Natural monopoly/oligopoly: market profitably supports only 1-3 players
- High fixed costs relative to market size discourage entry
- Evidence: stable market structure over 10+ years; failed entry attempts by well-funded competitors

### Step 2: Assess Moat Durability

For each identified moat type, evaluate:
- **Strengthening or weakening?** Is the moat wider today than 3 years ago?
- **What could erode it?** Technology shift, regulation change, competitor innovation?
- **Reinvestment required?** Does maintaining the moat require constant capex/R&D?

### Step 3: Score Each Moat Type

Rate each of the five moat types on a 0-100 scale:
- 0-20: No moat of this type
- 21-40: Weak — exists but easily challenged
- 41-60: Moderate — provides advantage but vulnerable to disruption
- 61-80: Strong — durable advantage with high barriers to erosion
- 81-100: Exceptional — world-class moat that has proven resilient across cycles

### Step 4: Calculate Moat Strength Score

Moat Strength = Pricing power score (25%) + Switching costs score (25%) + Network effects score (20%) + Cost advantage score (15%) + Intangible assets score (15%)

## Key Questions

1. Can you explain the moat in one sentence? If not, it may not exist.
2. If a well-funded competitor entered this market today with $1B, could they replicate this company's position within 5 years?
3. Is the moat widening (positive flywheel) or narrowing (competitive erosion)?
4. Has the moat survived a recession or industry downturn? Untested moats deserve a discount.
5. Does management actively invest in moat maintenance, or are they extracting value from a declining advantage?

## Red Flags

- Moat narrative relies on "brand" without evidence of pricing power (can they charge more than generics?)
- Single-source moat: one patent expiring, one regulation that could change, one key contract
- Moat requires constant reinvestment to maintain (not a moat — that's competitive necessity)
- Competitors growing faster despite the supposed moat
- Customer churn increasing despite high theoretical switching costs
- Network effects that haven't materialized into pricing power or margin expansion

## Source Requirements

- Competitive landscape: Tier 1 (SEC filings for market share, industry reports) + Tier 2 (industry news for context)
- Financial metrics (margins, ROIC, retention): Tier 1 (SEC filings, company IR)
- Customer data (churn, NPS, contract length): Tier 1 (company disclosures) or Tier 2 (surveys, reviews — qualitative only)
- Patent/IP data: Tier 1 (USPTO, SEC filings)

## Scoring

**Moat Strength Score (0-100)** = Pricing power (25%) + Switching costs (25%) + Network effects (20%) + Cost advantage (15%) + Intangible assets (15%)

Interpretation:
- 80-100: Exceptional moat — wide, durable, and strengthening
- 60-79: Strong moat — clear advantages with minor vulnerabilities
- 40-59: Moderate moat — some advantages but meaningful competitive risks
- 20-39: Weak moat — advantages exist but are narrow and vulnerable
- 0-19: No moat — competing on execution alone

## Output

- Moat Strength Score (0-100)
- Primary moat type(s) identified with evidence
- Per-type scores (0-100) for all five moat categories
- Moat trajectory: WIDENING / STABLE / NARROWING
- Top moat risk: what could erode this advantage?
- One-sentence moat summary
