## Purpose

Determine where the company's industry sits in its lifecycle. The lifecycle stage sets the baseline for what "good" looks like — a 15% revenue growth rate is exceptional in a mature industry but mediocre in a growth industry. Lifecycle stage also determines which valuation frameworks are appropriate and what competitive dynamics to expect.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Identify Lifecycle Stage

Classify the industry into one of four stages based on observable characteristics:

**Introduction**
- Few competitors, undefined market boundaries
- High R&D spend relative to revenue
- Negative or minimal profitability industry-wide
- Customer adoption is early — evangelism required
- No established pricing norms
- Typical industry growth: >30% annually

**Growth**
- Rapidly expanding customer base
- New entrants attracted by market opportunity
- Revenue growth significantly outpaces GDP
- Margins improving as scale is achieved
- Standards and market leaders beginning to emerge
- Typical industry growth: 15-30% annually

**Maturity**
- Growth slows to GDP-like rates
- Market share stabilizes among established players
- Competition shifts from growth to efficiency
- Consolidation through M&A
- Focus on cost optimization and shareholder returns
- Typical industry growth: 0-10% annually

**Decline**
- Shrinking total market due to substitution or obsolescence
- Excess capacity, price pressure
- Weak players exit; survivors extract remaining cash flows
- Innovation is minimal; capital is harvested not invested
- Typical industry growth: negative

### Step 2: Assess Transition Signals

Is the industry approaching a stage transition?
- Growth → Maturity signals: declining new customer growth, increasing M&A, margin plateaus
- Maturity → Decline signals: customer base shrinking, technology substitution, regulatory headwinds
- Decline → Renewal signals: new technology application, regulatory change, new use case discovery

### Step 3: Determine Implications

| Stage | Valuation Approach | Expected Growth | Competitive Focus |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Introduction | Revenue multiples, option value | >30% | Product-market fit |
| Growth | PEG, forward earnings | 15-30% | Market share capture |
| Maturity | P/E, dividend yield, FCF | 0-10% | Efficiency, returns |
| Decline | Asset value, liquidation | Negative | Cash extraction |

## Key Questions

1. What lifecycle stage is this industry in, and what does that imply for the company's growth ceiling?
2. Is there evidence of a stage transition in the next 3-5 years?
3. Is the company's growth rate consistent with its industry's stage? (Outperformance = share gains; underperformance = share losses)
4. Could technology or regulation reset the lifecycle? (e.g., AI making a mature industry feel like growth again)

## Red Flags

- Company valued on growth multiples in a mature industry
- Management projecting growth rates inconsistent with industry stage
- Industry showing decline signals but company narrative ignores them
- No evidence of adaptation strategy as industry transitions stages

## Source Requirements

- Industry growth rates: Tier 1 (licensed data providers, SEC filings for segment reporting)
- Competitive landscape: Tier 1 (SEC filings) + Tier 2 (industry news — qualitative only)
- Historical industry data: Tier 1 (government statistics, industry associations)

## Output

- Industry lifecycle stage: INTRODUCTION / GROWTH / MATURITY / DECLINE
- Transition signals: any indicators of approaching stage shift
- Stage-appropriate valuation framework recommendation
- Growth rate benchmark for the stage
- Company performance vs. stage expectations (OUTPERFORMING / IN-LINE / UNDERPERFORMING)
