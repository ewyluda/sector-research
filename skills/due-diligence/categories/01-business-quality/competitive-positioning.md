## Purpose

Determine where the company sits in its value chain and whether it captures or cedes economic value. A company can have a large market but occupy a weak position — understanding positioning reveals pricing power, margin sustainability, and vulnerability to disintermediation.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Map the Value Chain

Identify every link in the value chain from raw inputs to end customer:
- Suppliers → Manufacturer → Distributor → Retailer → Customer
- For services: Input providers → Platform/Service → Channel → End user

For each link, estimate: who captures the most margin? Where is pricing power concentrated?

### Step 2: Apply Porter's Five Forces (Adapted)

Evaluate each force on a 0-100 scale (0 = no threat, 100 = severe threat):

**Supplier Power**: Can suppliers raise prices without losing the company as a customer?
- Few suppliers = high power. Commodity inputs = low power.
- Key question: could the company switch suppliers within 90 days?

**Buyer Power**: Can customers negotiate prices down or switch easily?
- Concentrated buyer base = high power. Fragmented consumers = low power.
- Key question: does the top 10 customer concentration exceed 30%?

**Substitution Threat**: Are there alternative solutions (not competitors) that serve the same need?
- Different products solving the same problem. AI replacing human services. Open source replacing paid software.
- Key question: is the customer's problem solvable in a fundamentally different way?

**New Entrant Threat**: How easy is it for new competitors to enter?
- Capital requirements, regulatory barriers, network effects, brand loyalty.
- Key question: what would a new entrant need to invest before acquiring their first customer?

**Rivalry Intensity**: How aggressively do existing competitors compete?
- Price wars, feature races, customer acquisition spend.
- Key question: is the industry competing on value or on price?

### Step 3: Assess Value Capture Position

Classify the company's positioning:
- **Value Creator**: Makes the product/service that customers pay for (high capture potential)
- **Value Enabler**: Provides critical infrastructure others build on (moderate capture, high stickiness)
- **Value Distributor**: Connects creators to customers (capture depends on alternatives)
- **Value Extractor**: Monetizes a bottleneck (high capture but regulatory/disruption risk)

### Step 4: Calculate Position Score

Position Score = Market share trend (30%) + Relative margin vs. peers (25%) + Value chain power (25%) + Customer dependency inverse (20%)

## Key Questions

1. If this company disappeared tomorrow, how hard would it be for the ecosystem to replace it?
2. Does the company set prices or take prices? (Price setter = strong position)
3. Is the company moving up or down the value chain? (Up = capturing more; down = commoditizing)
4. How many links in the value chain could be disintermediated by technology?

## Red Flags

- Company is a price taker in a commodity market
- Top 3 customers represent >40% of revenue
- Value chain position is easily disintermediated (middleman without unique value-add)
- Competitors are vertically integrating and bypassing the company
- Margin compression while competitors' margins are stable or expanding

## Source Requirements

- Market share data: Tier 1 (SEC filings, industry reports from licensed providers)
- Revenue by customer/segment: Tier 1 (SEC filings — 10-K segment reporting)
- Peer margins: Tier 1 (SEC filings, FMP/Bloomberg)
- Value chain structure: Tier 2 (industry analysis — qualitative assessment)

## Scoring

**Position Score (0-100)** = Market share trend (30%) + Relative margin vs. peers (25%) + Value chain power (25%) + Customer dependency inverse (20%)

## Output

- Position Score (0-100)
- Value chain map showing company's position
- Five Forces assessment (per-force scores)
- Value capture classification: Creator / Enabler / Distributor / Extractor
- Positioning trajectory: STRENGTHENING / STABLE / WEAKENING
- Key positioning risk
