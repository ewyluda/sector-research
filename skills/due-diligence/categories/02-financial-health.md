## Purpose

Determine whether the business is financially sound, appropriately valued, and generating real cash. Financial health analysis separates companies that look good on the surface from those with durable, high-quality financial structures. A company can have a strong moat yet be priced to perfection — or appear cheap while harboring a deteriorating balance sheet.

This category bridges qualitative business analysis with quantitative financial reality.

## Key Dimensions

| Dimension | Sub-Skill | Core Question |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Valuation | valuation-multiples | Is the stock cheap or expensive relative to sector peers and its own history? |
| Intrinsic Value | dcf-analysis | What is the company worth under bear/base/bull assumptions? |
| Profitability | profitability-analysis | Is the business earning returns above its cost of capital, and is that improving? |
| Balance Sheet | balance-sheet-strength | Can the company survive adversity without a dilutive capital raise? |
| Cash Flows | cash-flow-quality | Is net income backed by real cash, or is accounting masking weakness? |

## Synthesis

After running all sub-skills, answer these integrative questions:
1. Is the **current price** justified by intrinsic value (DCF) and peer multiples (valuation-multiples)?
2. Is the company **creating or destroying** economic value (ROIC vs. WACC from profitability-analysis)?
3. Can the company **withstand a downturn** without existential balance sheet risk (Fortress Score)?
4. Are **earnings trustworthy** as a proxy for value creation (FCF conversion from cash-flow-quality)?

## Scoring

**FH Score (0-100)** = Valuation attractiveness (25%) + Profitability (25%) + Balance sheet (25%) + Cash flow quality (25%)

- Valuation attractiveness: derived from DCF discount to intrinsic value and relative multiple position; 80-100 = trading at >20% discount, 50-79 = fairly valued, 0-49 = expensive
- Profitability: ROIC vs. WACC spread and margin trend; scored qualitatively using profitability-analysis output
- Balance sheet: Fortress Score (0-100) directly from balance-sheet-strength
- Cash flow quality: FCF conversion rate and working capital trends; scored qualitatively using cash-flow-quality output

## Red Flags

- DCF intrinsic value (base case) >30% below current price with no growth catalyst to justify premium
- ROIC declining below WACC for 2+ consecutive years (value destruction accelerating)
- Fortress Score below 40 (high leverage, poor coverage, near-term maturity wall)
- FCF conversion below 60% of net income for 3+ consecutive quarters (earnings quality concern)
- P/E multiple contracting while EV/EBITDA remains elevated (hidden debt problem)
- Gross margin declining while operating margin holds flat (cost-shifting, not genuine improvement)

## Output

- FH Score (0-100) with sub-factor breakdown
- Fortress Score (0-100) from balance-sheet-strength
- DCF intrinsic value range (bear/base/bull)
- Valuation multiple summary vs. sector and 5-year history
- Profitability trend assessment (improving/stable/deteriorating)
- Cash flow quality rating with FCF conversion ratio
- Key financial health risks and monitoring triggers
