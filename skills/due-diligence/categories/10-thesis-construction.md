## Purpose

Thesis Construction is the synthesis layer of the due diligence framework. It is Phase 4 — it consumes the outputs of every prior category and converts them into an actionable investment thesis. The goal is not to produce one more analytical artifact; it is to produce a decision-ready document that can be defended, falsified, and monitored.

A well-constructed thesis does four things:
1. **States the bet explicitly** — What is the core claim? Why is the market wrong or incomplete?
2. **Assigns probabilities to outcomes** — Bull, base, and bear cases each have probability estimates that sum to ~100%.
3. **Names the catalysts** — What specific, time-bound events will prove or disprove the thesis?
4. **Grounds every claim in evidence** — No assertion floats free. Every quantitative claim traces to a specific data point from a prior sub-skill.

This category is the bridge between analysis and action. Analysts who skip it produce research that gets read and forgotten. Analysts who execute it well produce theses that generate returns.

## Synthesis

After running all sub-skills, answer these integrative questions:

1. **Internal consistency**: Do the bull and bear cases contradict each other on any factual claim? If so, resolve before finalizing.
2. **Catalyst coverage**: Does at least one catalyst exist for each of the bull and bear cases? A thesis with scenarios but no catalysts is not actionable.
3. **Variant quality**: Is the disagreement with consensus specific and falsifiable, or vague ("the market underappreciates the opportunity")? Vague variant perception is not alpha.
4. **Evidence coverage**: What percentage of thesis claims are supported by Tier 1 sources (10-K, audited financials, SEC filings)? Claims supported only by Tier 3 sources (analyst commentary, management guidance) require explicit flagging.
5. **Asymmetry check**: Does the risk/reward profile justify the position? If bull case target is 30% upside and bear case is 40% downside at equal probability, the thesis requires either conviction in probability asymmetry or a stop-loss discipline.

**The Core Question**: What has to be true for this investment to work, what has to be false for it to fail, and how will you know which is happening in real-time?

## Scoring

**This category has no composite score.** Thesis Construction is a process, not a measurement.

The prior nine categories produce scores (Business Quality, Financial Health, Growth & Earnings, Management & Governance, Technical & Market Structure, Macro Regime, Sentiment & Narrative, Risk Assessment, Future Durability). Those scores are inputs to the thesis — they inform the bull and bear probability estimates and help identify where the variant perception lives.

Thesis Construction itself is evaluated by quality criteria, not a numeric scale:
- Are the three scenarios explicit, with probability estimates summing to ~100%?
- Is every catalyst time-bound (quarter or year) and falsifiable?
- Is the variant perception specific enough to be proven wrong?
- Does every quantitative assertion cite a source tier and specific data point?

A thesis that passes all four criteria is ready for position sizing. One that fails any criterion requires additional work before deployment.

## Red Flags

- Bull and bear cases share the same core argument with only magnitude differences — this is not a real bull/bear framing, it is a sensitivity analysis
- No explicit probability estimates on scenarios — analyst is avoiding commitment, which means the position cannot be monitored
- Catalysts are described as "continued strong execution" or "macro tailwinds" — these are conditions, not events, and cannot be tracked as catalysts
- Variant perception is stated as "the market is not fully appreciating X" without specifying what consensus pricing implies and what your view implies
- More than 30% of quantitative assertions rely solely on management guidance (Tier 3) with no independent verification from Tier 1 or Tier 2 sources
- Bull case requires multiple independent things to go right simultaneously — compound probability is low; the thesis is implicitly more bearish than stated
- The thesis has no falsification criteria — if you cannot state what would make you wrong, the thesis is a narrative, not an investment thesis
- Base case price target is within 10% of current price — if the expected value case barely moves the stock, the thesis does not justify the research cost or position risk

## Output

- Bull case: core argument, 3-5 evidence points, probability estimate, price target / return
- Bear case: core argument, 3-5 evidence points, probability estimate, price target / return
- Base case: probability-weighted blend of bull and bear, expected return
- Catalyst list: minimum 3 catalysts (description, timeline, probability, magnitude, status)
- Variant perception statement: consensus view vs. thesis view, specific point of disagreement, evidence for/against
- Evidence map: every thesis claim linked to category, sub-skill, data point, and source tier
- Thesis status: CONVICTION / MONITOR / REVIEW (based on catalyst and evidence quality)
