## Purpose

Future Durability is the highest-priority investment category in this framework. Traditional due diligence evaluates a company's current position — this category asks the harder question: **will this business still exist, grow, and generate cash flows in 5, 10, and 15 years?**

The 2020s structural shift driven by AI makes this analysis non-optional. Companies with excellent fundamentals, strong moats, and growing revenues today may face existential disruption from AI-native competitors within a planning horizon relevant to long-term investors. Anthropic-class AI is already beginning to displace revenue streams at Salesforce, Adobe, Workday, Asana, and comparable enterprise software companies — not in theory, but in booking data, churn signals, and capital allocation shifts.

This category is organized around four questions:
1. **Cash Flow Durability**: What is the inherent durability of this company's revenue base at each time horizon?
2. **AI Disruption Vulnerability**: How exposed are the company's core revenue streams to AI substitution?
3. **Revenue Defensibility**: Do the company's moats hold against AI-native competition, or do traditional advantages dissolve?
4. **Technology Adoption Curve**: Where is AI adoption in this industry, and how much urgency does the company face?

## Synthesis

After running all sub-skills, answer these integrative questions:

1. **Horizon survivability**: Will this business generate more or less cash in 5 years? 10 years? 15 years? What is the probability of each?
2. **AI exposure concentration**: Is AI vulnerability spread across many small streams, or concentrated in 1-2 mission-critical revenue lines?
3. **Moat validity**: Does management understand that traditional moats may not protect against AI-native competitors? Are they investing in AI-native defensibility?
4. **Adaptation posture**: Is the company leading AI adoption in its industry, following, or ignoring it?
5. **Asymmetric risk**: Is the downside scenario (AI disruption) priced into the current valuation? Is the upside scenario (company becomes AI-native winner) realistic?

**The Existential Question**: If an AI-native startup launched today with $100M, which of this company's revenue streams would be gone in 5 years?

## Scoring

**FD Score (0-100)** = Durability-5yr Score (20%) + Durability-10yr Score (30%) + Durability-15yr Score (20%) + AI Vulnerability Inverse Score (30%)

AI Vulnerability Inverse Score = 100 − Vulnerability Score (so low vulnerability = high score)

**FD Score interpretation**:
- 80-100: Durable, AI-resilient business. Cash flows likely growing or stable at all horizons.
- 60-79: Moderate durability. Some AI exposure, but defensible core with adaptation optionality.
- 40-59: Material risk. Significant AI disruption exposure at 5-10yr horizon. Position sizing discipline required.
- 20-39: High disruption risk. Multiple revenue streams vulnerable. Requires explicit AI adaptation thesis to hold.
- 0-19: Existential risk. Core business model replaceable by AI at scale. Consider short thesis or deep value only.

**Sub-score weighting rationale**: The 10yr Durability score receives the highest weight because it captures the full effect of AI substitution (which takes 3-7 years to play out at scale) while still being within a realistic planning horizon. AI Vulnerability Inverse receives 30% because it is the most differentiated and least-priced factor in current market valuations.

## Red Flags

- Revenue heavily concentrated in cognitive routine tasks (document processing, data entry, content generation, customer support, code generation) — these are highest-risk AI substitution targets
- No articulated AI strategy from management or strategy is purely defensive ("we'll integrate AI into our product") rather than offensive
- AI-native competitors already shipping and taking market share in the company's core segment
- High gross margins on services that AI can deliver at near-zero marginal cost (a margin that AI erodes, not protects)
- Moat analysis reveals advantages rooted solely in information asymmetry, expertise aggregation, or manual process — all of which AI directly attacks
- Customer base is tech-forward and price-sensitive — the cohort most likely to switch to AI-native alternatives first
- Recurring revenue relies on contracts with 1-3 year terms — rollover risk becomes AI substitution risk at renewal
- Management dismisses AI disruption risk without a substantive counter-argument
- Industry peers are already reporting AI-driven churn or elongated sales cycles attributable to "wait and see" buyer behavior
- No proprietary data flywheel — company cannot train AI on unique data that competitors cannot access

## Output

- FD Score (0-100) with sub-component breakdown
- Durability score at 5yr, 10yr, and 15yr horizons
- AI Vulnerability Score (0-100) with per-stream breakdown
- Revenue defensibility classification (STRONG / MODERATE / WEAK) against AI-native competition
- Technology adoption curve position (Early / Growth / Mature / Saturated) with urgency rating
- Top 3 AI disruption scenarios with probability and revenue impact estimates
- Recommended monitoring triggers (what signals would confirm or refute the disruption thesis)
