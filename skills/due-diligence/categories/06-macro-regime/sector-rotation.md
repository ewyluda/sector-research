## Purpose

Different sectors of the economy and equity market outperform at different stages of the economic cycle. This is not random — it reflects the sequence in which economic activity accelerates and decelerates across business and consumer spending, credit availability, inventory cycles, and policy response. Understanding where the economy sits in its cycle and which sectors have historically outperformed that phase allows an investor to assess whether the current investment is aligned with or fighting the macro rotation.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Identify the Economic Cycle Phase

Classify the current environment into one of four phases using the following indicators:

**EARLY CYCLE (Recovery)**
- GDP growth: turning positive after contraction; often negative YoY but accelerating
- ISM Manufacturing PMI: rising from below 50 toward 50-55 range
- Credit conditions: easing; banks beginning to loosen lending standards
- Unemployment: high but peaking; initial jobless claims beginning to decline
- Fed policy: easy (rates low, accommodation in place)
- Consumer spending: beginning to recover; housing and durables lead
- Corporate earnings: EPS trough passed; beats becoming more common
- Yield curve: steepening sharply as short rates stay low and growth expectations build

**MID CYCLE (Expansion)**
- GDP growth: above trend; solid quarterly gains
- ISM Manufacturing PMI: 55-60 range; sustained expansion
- Credit conditions: normal; broad availability
- Unemployment: declining steadily
- Fed policy: neutral to beginning early normalization
- Consumer spending: broad-based; services spending picks up
- Corporate earnings: strong growth; guidance being raised
- Yield curve: normal slope, mildly steepening

**LATE CYCLE (Peak)**
- GDP growth: above trend but decelerating at the margin
- ISM Manufacturing PMI: 55-65 range but flattening or starting to decline
- Credit conditions: tightening; lending standards beginning to tighten
- Unemployment: very low (at or below full employment); wage growth accelerating
- Fed policy: restrictive; active hiking cycle; real rates positive
- Consumer spending: still strong but showing some fatigue in rate-sensitive areas
- Corporate earnings: growing but beat rates declining; guidance cautious
- Yield curve: flattening or beginning to invert

**RECESSION (Contraction)**
- GDP growth: negative; official NBER recession or imminent
- ISM Manufacturing PMI: below 50; often 45-48 range
- Credit conditions: tight; lending standards restrictive; credit spreads wide
- Unemployment: rising rapidly
- Fed policy: pivoting to cuts; emergency easing possible
- Consumer spending: declining across the board; services hold longer than goods
- Corporate earnings: negative growth; EPS cuts; guidance withdrawn
- Yield curve: inverted then beginning to steepen (the actual recession signal)

### Step 2: Cross-Reference Multiple Indicators

No single indicator determines cycle phase. Use a weight-of-evidence approach:

| Indicator | Early | Mid | Late | Recession |
|-----------|-------|-----|------|-----------|
| ISM PMI | <50 rising | 55-60 | 60+ then falling | <50 falling |
| 3M10Y curve | Steepening | Normal | Flattening/Inverting | Inverted/Steepening |
| Leading indicators (LEI) | Rising | Rising | Plateauing/Falling | Falling |
| Credit spreads | Tightening from wide | Tight | Tightening/Stable | Widening |
| Unemployment | High/Peaking | Declining | Very low | Rising fast |
| Fed policy | Easy/unchanged | Neutral/Early hike | Active hiking | Cutting |

If 4 of 6 indicators align to a phase, classify with HIGH confidence. 3 of 6 = MODERATE. Fewer = TRANSITION.

### Step 3: Map Cycle Phase to Historical Sector Performance

Historical sector outperformance by cycle phase (based on analysis of post-WWII U.S. economic cycles):

**EARLY CYCLE outperformers:**
- Technology: leveraged to capex recovery; beaten down in recession
- Consumer Discretionary: pent-up demand release; rate-sensitive recovery beneficiary
- Industrials: infrastructure and manufacturing capex leads recoveries
- Financials: credit spreads tighten; loan growth accelerates; NIM benefits

**MID CYCLE outperformers:**
- Technology: continued capex expansion; earnings growth strong
- Communication Services: consumer spending on services expands
- Financials: loan growth peaks; NIM stable; credit quality excellent
- Industrials: continued capex momentum
- Materials: raw material demand peaks in mid cycle

**LATE CYCLE outperformers:**
- Energy: commodity prices elevated as global demand peaks
- Healthcare: defensive rotation begins; secular demand from aging demographics
- Utilities: bond proxy demand as rates approach peak; defensive reallocation
- Consumer Staples: non-discretionary demand; pricing power in inflation
- Materials: still benefiting from elevated commodity prices

**RECESSION outperformers:**
- Utilities: highest defensive quality; regulated earnings; dividends
- Consumer Staples: necessity demand; pricing power maintained; dividends
- Healthcare: non-cyclical demand; government-backed reimbursement (partial)
- Treasuries / Cash: capital preservation phase

### Step 4: Assess Company Alignment with Cycle Phase

Determine whether the company being analyzed sits in a historically favorable or unfavorable sector for the current cycle phase:

- **Aligned (favorable)**: company's sector is historically a top-2 performer in the current cycle phase
- **Neutral**: sector historically mid-pack; neither materially helped nor hurt
- **Misaligned (unfavorable)**: company's sector historically underperforms in the current cycle phase

Then add company-specific qualifiers:
- Does the company have idiosyncratic drivers that could override sector rotation patterns (e.g., a turnaround, new product cycle)?
- Is the company a sector leader or laggard? Leaders often outperform even when sector rotation is unfavorable.
- What does management guidance suggest about near-term demand trends relative to historical cycle patterns?

### Step 5: Verify with Current Relative Performance

Check whether the current rotation in equity markets is consistent with the identified cycle phase:
- Which sectors have outperformed the S&P 500 over the past 3 and 6 months?
- Is the market rotation consistent with the fundamental cycle identification, or are there anomalies?
- Anomalies can signal: (a) cycle phase is misidentified, (b) structural forces are overriding cyclical patterns, or (c) an impending phase transition

## Key Questions

1. Where is the ISM Manufacturing PMI — below 50 (contraction), 50-55 (early expansion), 55-60 (mid expansion), above 60 or declining sharply (late cycle)?
2. Are leading indicators (LEI) rising or falling? The Conference Board LEI is a composite that leads the cycle by 6-9 months.
3. Is the sector historically a top-3 performer in the current cycle phase?
4. If the sector is misaligned with the cycle phase, what is the idiosyncratic thesis that overrides the historical pattern?
5. Is actual sector rotation in the equity market consistent with the identified cycle phase?

## Red Flags

- Investing in a historically late-cycle or recession-phase sector (Utilities, Staples) when cycle indicators point to early recovery — opportunity cost and likely underperformance
- Investing in a historically early-cycle sector (Tech, Discretionary) when ISM is rolling over and yield curve is inverted — catching a falling knife
- More than half of leading indicators deteriorating — cycle transition likely within 6-12 months
- Sector rotation in actual equity markets contradicts the identified cycle phase — reassess cycle identification
- Company's management guiding conservatively on volume/demand at a phase of the cycle where acceleration is expected — company-specific issue, not just macro

## Source Requirements

- **ISM Manufacturing PMI**: Institute for Supply Management (ismworld.org) — Tier 1
- **Conference Board Leading Economic Index (LEI)**: Conference Board (conference-board.org) — Tier 1
- **GDP growth rate**: Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) — Tier 1
- **Unemployment rate and initial claims**: Bureau of Labor Statistics, Department of Labor — Tier 1
- **Credit spreads**: FRED (BAMLH0A0HYM2) — Tier 1
- **Sector relative performance**: Bloomberg, Koyfin, or S&P GICS sector ETF performance data — Tier 2
- **Historical cycle analysis**: Federal Reserve research, academic studies, Fidelity sector cycle research — Tier 2

## Scoring

**Sector Rotation Alignment Score (0-100)** based on how well the investment aligns with the current cycle phase:

- 80-100: Sector is a top-2 historical performer in the current cycle phase; company is a sector leader; actual rotation confirms
- 60-79: Sector is mid-pack; company has idiosyncratic drivers that partially offset; rotation broadly aligned
- 40-59: Sector is mildly misaligned OR cycle phase is uncertain (TRANSITION); mixed signals
- 20-39: Sector historically underperforms in the current phase; no strong idiosyncratic offset
- 0-19: Sector is the worst historical performer in the current cycle phase (e.g., growth/tech in recession); full cycle headwind

## Output

- Sector Rotation Alignment Score (0-100)
- Economic cycle phase: EARLY / MID / LATE / RECESSION with confidence level (HIGH / MODERATE / LOW)
- Key indicators driving phase identification (ISM, LEI, yield curve, unemployment, credit spreads)
- Historical sector performance ranking for identified phase (where does this sector fall?)
- Company alignment assessment: ALIGNED / NEUTRAL / MISALIGNED with rationale
- Idiosyncratic override factors (if any) that could decouple company from sector rotation
- Current market rotation check: is actual equity sector performance consistent with identified cycle phase?
