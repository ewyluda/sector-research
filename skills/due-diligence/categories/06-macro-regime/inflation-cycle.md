## Purpose

Inflation is not static — it moves through identifiable cycles of rising, peaking, falling, and bottoming. Each phase creates different winners and losers at the company level. The critical question is not just "is inflation high?" but "can this company pass inflation through to customers, or will it compress margins?" Understanding cycle position and company-specific pricing power determines whether inflation is a headwind, neutral, or even a tailwind.

## Methodology

### Step 1: Establish Inflation Level and Trajectory

Gather key inflation data:
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: headline and core (ex-food, energy); month-over-month and year-over-year
- **PPI (Producer Price Index)**: headline and core; measures upstream input cost pressure
- **PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures)**: Fed's preferred inflation measure; core PCE is most policy-relevant
- **Wage growth**: Average Hourly Earnings (AHE) growth rate; wages are the largest input cost for most businesses
- **5-year/5-year forward breakeven**: market's expectation of long-run inflation; signals whether inflation is seen as transitory or entrenched

### Step 2: Classify the Inflation Cycle Phase

Map current data to one of four cycle phases:

**RISING** — CPI/PPI accelerating month-over-month; breakevens moving higher; wage growth accelerating
- Characteristics: input costs rising faster than companies can pass through; early-phase margin compression; Fed likely to tighten
- Equity impact: value/cyclicals initially benefit; growth/long-duration hurt by rising rates concurrent with inflation

**PEAKING** — CPI/PPI decelerating at the margin but still elevated; breakevens stabilizing; Fed at or near terminal rate
- Characteristics: pricing power of companies peaks here; sequential cost growth stabilizes but base effects keep YoY elevated
- Equity impact: highest uncertainty phase; market debates whether peak is in or whether inflation re-accelerates

**FALLING** — CPI/PPI clearly decelerating; breakevens declining; wage growth moderating; Fed pivoting or signaling cuts
- Characteristics: companies with pricing power locked in retain elevated prices while input costs fall — margin expansion opportunity
- Equity impact: relief for growth assets; duration re-rates positively as rates fall alongside inflation

**BOTTOMING** — CPI/PPI near target (2% PCE) or below; breakevens stable to ticking up; economy potentially re-accelerating
- Characteristics: inflation fears fade; growth concerns dominate; base effects begin to push YoY inflation slightly higher
- Equity impact: most benign for equities historically; low inflation + growth = highest P/E expansion environment

### Step 3: Assess Company-Specific Pricing Power

Determine whether the company can pass inflation through to customers:

**Strong pricing power indicators:**
- Price increases implemented without meaningful volume loss (evidence from earnings transcripts or management commentary)
- Products/services with no close substitutes or high switching costs
- Long-term contracts with inflation escalation clauses
- Pricing tied to indices (CPI-linked contracts)
- Gross margins stable or expanding despite rising input costs

**Weak pricing power indicators:**
- Company operates in commodity or near-commodity markets (price-taker, not price-maker)
- Customers are highly price-sensitive and have alternatives
- Recent volume losses following price increases
- Gross margins compressing even as revenue grows (revenue growth is price-driven but costs are growing faster)
- Management commentary focusing on "absorbing" rather than "passing through" cost increases

### Step 4: Analyze Cost Structure Exposure

Beyond revenue pricing, assess the cost base:
- **Labor-intensive businesses**: high exposure to wage inflation; difficult to automate quickly
- **Energy-intensive businesses**: input cost directly tied to energy prices, which can be volatile
- **Commodity-input businesses**: margin squeeze during commodity spikes unless hedged
- **Asset-light / software businesses**: low input cost inflation; primarily exposed through wage growth for talent

Map each major cost line to its inflation sensitivity:
- Cost of goods sold (COGS): what portion is materials, energy, labor?
- SG&A: what portion is people costs vs. fixed overhead?
- Are there hedge programs in place? How long is the hedge book?

### Step 5: Assess Sector-Level Inflation Winners and Losers

| Sector | Inflation Impact | Reason |
|--------|-----------------|--------|
| Energy | Positive | Revenue tied to commodity prices that drive inflation |
| Materials | Positive | Input prices are the revenue line |
| Real Estate | Mildly positive | Rents can be inflation-linked; asset values hedge inflation |
| Financials | Mixed | Rates rise with inflation (positive for NIM) but loan quality risks |
| Consumer Staples | Mixed | Pricing power + necessity demand, but input costs also rise |
| Technology (SaaS) | Mildly positive | High margins + low input cost inflation; wage pressure is primary risk |
| Consumer Discretionary | Negative | Discretionary demand falls as real incomes compress |
| Utilities | Negative | Regulated pricing; cost pass-through lagged or capped by regulators |
| Healthcare (services) | Mixed | Reimbursement rates often fixed; wage inflation hard to pass through |

## Key Questions

1. What is the CPI and PPI trend over the last 3, 6, and 12 months — is the rate of change accelerating or decelerating?
2. Has the company successfully raised prices in the past 12-18 months, and what was the volume/unit response?
3. What percentage of COGS is tied to commodities, energy, or labor — the three primary inflation vectors?
4. Does the company have long-term contracts with fixed pricing, and when do they reprice?
5. Are breakevens stable (inflation priced in) or rising (inflation surprise risk still elevated)?

## Red Flags

- CPI re-accelerating after appearing to peak — "last mile" inflation problem; prolongs rate pressure
- Company raised prices aggressively but is now seeing volume deterioration — pricing power was temporary
- Labor-intensive business facing minimum wage increases across multiple jurisdictions simultaneously
- No hedging program in place for major commodity inputs during a RISING phase
- Management claiming pricing power without evidence (gross margins flat or declining despite price increases)
- PPI running hotter than CPI — input costs rising faster than selling prices; margin compression is mathematical
- Company's long-term contracts have no inflation escalation clauses and reprice slowly

## Source Requirements

- **CPI and PCE data**: Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), Federal Reserve FRED — Tier 1
- **PPI data**: Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) — Tier 1
- **Wage growth (AHE)**: Bureau of Labor Statistics monthly Employment Situation report — Tier 1
- **Breakeven inflation rates**: FRED (T5YIE, T10YIE) — Tier 1
- **Company pricing actions**: earnings call transcripts, investor presentations — Tier 1
- **Gross margin trends**: SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q income statements) — Tier 1
- **Cost structure breakdown**: SEC filings, management discussion and analysis — Tier 1

## Scoring

**Inflation Impact Score (0-100)** based on cycle phase and company-specific positioning:

- 80-100: Company is a clear inflation winner (energy/materials or has strong pricing power + falling inflation cycle)
- 60-79: Neutral to mildly positive (inflation falling, company has adequate pricing power to protect margins)
- 40-59: Mixed (some pricing power, some exposure; margin impact is uncertain)
- 20-39: Meaningful headwind (rising inflation, limited pricing power, cost-exposed business model)
- 0-19: Severe headwind (rising inflation + price-taker + labor/commodity intensive + margin compression visible)

## Output

- Inflation Impact Score (0-100)
- Inflation cycle phase: RISING / PEAKING / FALLING / BOTTOMING with supporting data points
- Current CPI, core CPI, PCE, PPI, and wage growth levels with 6-month trend
- 5-year/5-year forward breakeven: current level and direction
- Company pricing power assessment: STRONG / MODERATE / WEAK with evidence
- Cost structure inflation exposure: which cost lines are most at risk and by how much
- Margin impact estimate: quantified directional impact on gross and operating margins given current cycle phase
