## Purpose

Determine whether the business has structural advantages that protect margins, market share, and pricing power over time. Business quality is the foundation of long-term investing — a cheap stock with no moat is a value trap, and a growing company in a declining industry is borrowing time.

## Key Dimensions

| Dimension | Sub-Skill | Core Question |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Moat | moat-analysis | Can competitors replicate this business in 5 years? |
| Positioning | competitive-positioning | Where does the company sit in its value chain, and does it capture or cede value? |
| Market Size | tam-market-sizing | Is the addressable market growing, stable, or shrinking? |
| Lifecycle | industry-lifecycle | Is this industry in growth, maturity, or decline? |

## Synthesis

After running all sub-skills, answer these integrative questions:
1. Is this a **good business** (high ROIC, durable moat, growing TAM)?
2. Is it getting **better or worse** (moat widening/narrowing, share gaining/losing)?
3. What would **break** the business quality thesis?

## Scoring

**BQ Score (0-100)** = Moat Strength (40%) + Position Score (30%) + TAM trend (15%) + Lifecycle stage (15%)

TAM trend scoring: Growing TAM = 70-100, Stable TAM = 40-60, Shrinking TAM = 0-30.
Lifecycle scoring: Introduction = 50, Growth = 80, Maturity = 50, Decline = 20.

## Red Flags

- ROIC declining toward cost of capital over 3+ years
- Market share losses in core segments for 2+ consecutive periods
- Moat dependent on a single factor (one patent, one regulation, one contract)
- Industry in secular decline with no visible pivot optionality
- Gross margins compressing while competitors' margins expand

## Output

- BQ Score (0-100) with sub-factor breakdown
- Moat type classification and durability assessment
- Competitive position map (where value accrues in the chain)
- TAM estimate with growth trajectory
- Industry lifecycle stage
- Key business quality risks and monitoring triggers
